Provisional Ratings Guide

Don't panic if your child's rating wildly jumps up and down after their first few tournaments. This is normal and is known as a "provisional" rating.

What does "Provisional" mean?

In the US Chess Federation, a player's rating is considered "provisional" until they have completed 26 rated games. You will often see this denoted with a "P" or the number of games played next to their score (e.g., 600 P12).

Why does it change so fast?

Because the system doesn't have a lot of data on your child yet, provisional ratings use a special mathematical formula that allows for massive swings. A single good tournament could bump their score by 300 points, while a bad one could drop it just as fast. This helps the system quickly find their true skill level.

Parent Tip: Focus on the Games, Not the Number

During these first 26 games, do not let your child obsess over their rating number. Emphasize learning, recording their games properly, and having fun!
